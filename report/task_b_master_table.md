### Comparative Family Analysis: How Algorithmic Families Differ

Across the 37 algorithms investigated in this laboratory catalogue, the structural mechanisms for managing **diversity, convergence, constraints, and non-convex Pareto fronts** differ fundamentally across algorithmic families:

1. **Diversity Handling:**
   - *Classical methods (1–7)* achieve diversity solely through extrinsic parameter sweeps (varying weights, $\varepsilon$ bounds, or goals). They have no built-in awareness of point spread.
   - *Non-elitist evolutionary methods (10–13)* rely on artificial phenotypic or genotypic fitness sharing ($\sigma_{share}$), which requires manual radius tuning and suffers from genetic drift.
   - *Elitist algorithms (14, 16, 17)* introduce parameterless intrinsic diversity operators: NSGA-II uses crowding distance in objective space, SPEA2 uses $k$-th nearest neighbor density, and PESA-II introduces region-based hyperbox selection.
   - *Decomposition methods (18–20)* enforce diversity structurally by projecting the search along predefined weight vectors across localized neighborhoods.
   - *Many-Objective algorithms (15, 31, 34)* replace crowding distance with Das & Dennis reference lines (NSGA-III), Angle-Penalized Distance (RVEA), or Monte-Carlo hypervolume slicing (HypE) to avoid hyperspace boundary collapse.

2. **Convergence Enforcement:**
   - *Classical mathematical programming* converges deterministically using gradient-based KKT stationary updates (SLSQP), achieving rapid convergence when subproblems are convex.
   - *Non-elitist algorithms (VEGA, MOGA, NSGA, NPGA)* lack survival guarantees; high-performing compromise solutions can be eliminated in subsequent generations, leading to drifting fronts.
   - *Elitist metaheuristics (NSGA-II, SPEA2, MODE, MOBA)* employ monotonic $(N+N)$ survival truncation or external non-dominated archives, strictly preserving the Pareto frontier.
   - *Indicator-based methods (IBEA, SMS-EMOA)* use continuous mathematical quality indicators ($\varepsilon$-indicator, exact hypervolume contribution) to guarantee monotonic hypervolume ascent.

3. **Constraint Handling:**
   - *Penalty Methods (Classical 1–7):* Aggregate constraint violations into scalar penalty terms $\Phi(x) = f(x) + \rho \sum \max(0, g_j(x))$, requiring sensitive multiplier calibration.
   - *Deb's Feasibility-First Rules (Constrained NSGA-II, Alg 21):* Parameter-free comparison rules where feasible strictly dominates infeasible, and lower constraint violation wins among infeasible candidates.
   - *Swarm and Evolutionary Clamping:* Algorithms like MOPSO, MOGWO, and MOBA clamp boundary coordinates and discard infeasible local walk candidates.

4. **Non-Convex Pareto Fronts:**
   - *Weighted Sum Failure:* As verified on Problem P3 (ZDT2), linear scalarization is fundamentally incapable of reaching the interior of dented/non-convex Pareto fronts, collapsing to the extreme vertices ($HV = 0.21, IGD = 0.3543$).
   - *$\varepsilon$-Constraint & Tchebycheff Success:* Bounding auxiliary objectives or minimizing Chebyshev distances cleanly penetrates inward-curving geometries.
   - *Pareto Dominance Metaheuristics:* Population-based Pareto sorting (NSGA-II, MODE, IBEA, MOEA/D-DE) operates without convexity assumptions, capturing arbitrary discontinuous and concave manifolds.
